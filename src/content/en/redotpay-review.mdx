People searching for a RedotPay review usually want three answers: what you are actually buying, what it costs, and whether the account will open. This page stays inside what RedotPay has published. Figures were checked on 24 October 2026. If the app disagrees, the app wins, and we update the page when we re-read the sources.

## What RedotPay is

RedotPay’s services page describes one app that does two jobs:

1. A **wallet** that receives crypto. The published list is USDT, USDC, BTC, ETH, BNB, SOL, TRX, XRP, SUI, and S. [Services and features](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/11070044-redotpay-services-and-features) (page date 24 July 2026).
2. A **card** tied to that wallet. The [card terms](https://www.redotpay.com/terms/redotpay-card) name a Hong Kong programme (Visa) and a Singapore programme (Visa). The [Singapore product page](https://www.redotpay.com/sg) says the card is issued by Xfers Pte Ltd under a Visa Worldwide licence. The programme depends on where you live. The newer [where-to-use article](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/11027583-where-can-i-use-my-redotpay-card) (4 September 2026) talks about major card networks and 130 million+ merchants. We do not describe the card as Mastercard.

At the checkout, the merchant sees a card payment. The app converts crypto in the background. The ATM help page publishes a **1%** crypto-conversion rate, and it does not say whether that rate applies to every purchase or only to withdrawals. That limit is spelled out on the [fees page](/en/articles/redotpay-fees).

## Virtual card and physical card

| | Virtual | Physical |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | Card numbers in the app | A plastic card in the post |
| When | After verification and issuance | After delivery. No fixed transit time is published |
| Online | Yes | Yes |
| Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal | The [payments article](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/11027588-how-do-i-use-my-redotpay-card-for-payments) says a virtual card can be linked. A wallet can still decline it | The same help page is about using the card, not a promise for every region |
| In-store | Through the phone, if the wallet accepts the card | Yes, with the plastic |
| ATM | No | Yes, where major card networks are accepted |
| Issuance | 10 USD once | 100 USD once. The order steps do not list a separate postage fee, which is not the same as “shipping is free” |

If you only pay online, the virtual card is the cheaper start. ATM cash requires the physical card, and some addresses never receive one. Read the [country lists](/en/articles/redotpay-supported-countries) before you pay 100 USD.

## What you can deposit

The services page (24 July 2026) lists **USDT, USDC, BTC, ETH, BNB, SOL, TRX, XRP, SUI, and S**. The deposit article says each asset is accepted on networks shown inside the app, and it does not publish one network table in the help text. Sending on a network the deposit address does not use can lose the funds. Steps are in [how to top up RedotPay](/en/articles/how-to-top-up-redotpay).

A balance held in BTC can move before you spend it. USDT and USDC are the assets people usually keep for spending because they are meant to track the dollar. That is a product choice, not a guarantee the peg holds.

## Where the card works, and where the account does not

Acceptance and eligibility are different questions.

The where-to-use article says you can pay online, in person, and with a physical card at ATMs that support major card networks, at 130 million+ merchants. It also says some merchants block prepaid or crypto-linked cards, and the card is not for sanctioned places.

Eligibility is a set of block lists, not a welcome list. US residents cannot be issued a card. People located in the United States cannot open the app. US citizens cannot finish KYC. The same pages name other countries, including parts of Europe. Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria, and the Gulf states we checked are not on those lists. India is on the card-issuance list, so Indian residents are ineligible for a card. A missing name is not an approval. The names are copied in the [country guide](/en/articles/redotpay-supported-countries).

## What is attractive, and what is not

**Useful when it fits**

- You spend crypto without first selling on an exchange and waiting for a bank transfer.
- After KYC, a virtual card is 10 USD and works online.
- The payments help page documents Apple Pay, Google Pay, and PayPal for the virtual card, with the caveat above.
- You can deposit from an exchange or a wallet you already use. You do not have to trade on RedotPay.

**Limits you should price in**

- The balance is custodial. RedotPay holds the assets.
- The physical card is 100 USD, and shipping is not published as its own line.
- ATM withdrawals are priced separately and are usually the expensive way to use the card. See the [ATM guide](/en/articles/redotpay-atm-withdrawal).
- There is no single published spend cap for every account. The app shows the limit that applies to you.
- The 5 USD sign-up reward is spending credit for 30 days. It cannot pay issuance or replacement. It is not tied to a referral code. How to enter a code is in the [referral guide](/en/articles/redotpay-referral-code).

## Who it is for

It is a practical card if RedotPay will verify your citizenship, you are comfortable leaving spending money with a custodian, and you have checked the fee table against how you actually pay. It is a poor fit if you need a bank account replacement, a credit line, or a card in a country on the block list. Compare the shape of other products, without invented competitor fees, on the [comparison page](/en/articles/redotpay-vs-other-crypto-cards).
