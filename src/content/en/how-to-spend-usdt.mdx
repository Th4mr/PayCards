“How to spend USDT” usually means one of three jobs: pay a website, pay in a shop, or get cash. This page is about the first two. Cash has its own guide, [convert USDT to cash](/en/articles/convert-usdt-to-cash), because a virtual card cannot do it.

USDT is a token meant to track the dollar. A supermarket till does not take the token. Something in the middle has to turn it into a payment the merchant already accepts.

Checked on **24 October 2026**.

## Three legitimate paths

1. **A crypto card.** You deposit USDT, and the card pays the merchant. RedotPay is this kind of app. You do not need an exchange account for it.
2. **An exchange card.** If the USDT already sits on an exchange that issues cards, that company’s card page is the place to read the rules. [Bybit’s introduction to Bybit Card](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Introduction-to-Bybit-Card) (updated 10 June 2026) and [Crypto.com’s card page](https://crypto.com/en/cards/) are the official starting points. We are not copying their fees, and we are not describing how Bybit funds its card.
3. **Sell, then use a bank card.** You convert USDT to fiat on an exchange or with a person you trust, withdraw to a bank, and pay as usual. That can be slower and it can be the only path if no card will be issued where you live. P2P ads and “USDT to cash near me” are marketplaces, not a rate we publish.

## The RedotPay version

[RedotPay services and features](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/11070044-redotpay-services-and-features) (24 July 2026) lists USDT among the assets the wallet can hold. [Supported cryptocurrencies, networks, and minimum deposits](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/10693364-supported-cryptocurrencies-networks-minimum-deposits) (21 July 2026) publishes a minimums image, including 1.00 USDT on each listed network. Confirm the row on the Deposit screen. The table is in [how to top up](/en/articles/how-to-top-up-redotpay).

Send USDT only on a network the app is showing for that address. A deposit below the minimum, after network fees, is not credited. A later deposit of the same asset on the same network can be combined with it and then credited. The deposit article does not say a wrong-network transfer can be combined. The steps are in [how to top up](/en/articles/how-to-top-up-redotpay).

Then the card spends the balance. Online, the [virtual card](/en/articles/virtual-card-online-payments) is 10 USD once. In person, you either use a wallet that accepted the virtual card, or the physical card. The [where-to-use article](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/11027583-where-can-i-use-my-redotpay-card) (4 September 2026) says 130 million+ merchants on major card networks, online and in person, and that some merchants block prepaid or crypto-linked cards.

So “spend USDT at Amazon”, “on AliExpress”, or “on a subscription” is a maybe. The [merchant guide](/en/articles/redotpay-netflix-chatgpt) is the page that refuses to turn customer anecdotes into a guarantee.

## What this does not do

- It does not turn USDT into a bank transfer of RedotPay’s USD balance. The withdraw article says USDs cannot leave to an external wallet or a bank. EUR or GBP can go to a linked account in the same currency. That is [withdraw to a bank](/en/articles/redotpay-withdraw-bank-paypal), not everyday spending.
- It does not cash out to Vodafone Cash, Sham Cash, Zain Cash, or a similar local wallet. Those phrases show up in search. They are not on the RedotPay pages we read.
- It does not skip identity checks. See [crypto card no KYC](/en/articles/crypto-card-no-kyc).
- It does not ignore country lists. US residents are ineligible. A missing country is not an approval. Use the [country guide](/en/articles/redotpay-supported-countries).

The ATM page’s **1%** crypto-conversion line is real, and it is not a blanket receipt for every purchase. Another currency can be a different percentage. Read the [fees guide](/en/articles/redotpay-fees) before you assume the shop price is the only price.

<CTABox lang="en" />

If the USDT is for a website, start with the virtual card and a small payment. If the payment matters, keep a second way to pay. The card is one option, not a promise.
