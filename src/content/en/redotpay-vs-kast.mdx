People search RedotPay and KAST together because both spend crypto. The products are not the same shape, and a fee on one site is not a fee on the other. Checked on **25 October 2026**.

## What each company is, from its own pages

**RedotPay** is a spending app. You deposit crypto, then the card spends it. The [review](/en/articles/redotpay-review) quotes the card description. US citizens cannot finish KYC. People located in the United States cannot open the app. US residents cannot be issued a virtual or a physical card. The lists are in the [country guide](/en/articles/redotpay-supported-countries).

**KAST** publishes card and account fees on [What are the fees and conditions for KAST cards and accounts?](https://concierge.kast.xyz/hc/en-us/articles/9850062738703-What-Are-the-Fees-and-Conditions-for-KAST-Cards-and-Accounts) (updated 22 October 2026) and memberships on [What are KAST memberships and how do they work?](https://concierge.kast.xyz/hc/en-us/articles/15806136679695-What-Are-KAST-Memberships-and-How-Do-They-Work) (updated 10 September 2026). This page does not turn KAST’s “Global” label into a list of countries.

## Compare eligibility before the table

1. Can the account open, and can a card be issued, where you live? RedotPay’s answer is the block lists. KAST’s answer is on KAST’s site. We did not copy a KAST country list.
2. How you fund the balance. RedotPay’s deposit minimums are in [how to top up](/en/articles/how-to-top-up-redotpay). KAST’s stablecoin deposit line is below.
3. The fee you actually hit: issuance, FX, conversion, ATM, or a membership. A 0% spend line does not cancel a membership or an FX band.

## RedotPay’s published fees

<FeeTable lang="en" variant="compact" />

Two rows sit on the full [fees page](/en/articles/redotpay-fees) and are not in the compact table. Both are in section 2, “Transactions”, of the [limitations and fees chart](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/10339271-redotpay-card-limitations-fees) (page updated 24 July 2026). The small-transaction line on the fees-table image is marked effective 1 December 2025.

- **Small transaction:** the first 5 transactions per card per month of 1.00 USD or less are waived. After that, 0.2 USD. That is a small authorization, not a decline fee.
- **Declined transaction:** the first 3 declined transactions are waived, then 0.5 USD. The chart’s examples include insufficient balance, a frozen card, a lost card, and incorrect information. The list is not exhaustive.

The 1% crypto-conversion fee and the 1.2% fee when the transaction currency is not the card’s default currency are in that same section 2. They are general transaction fees. They are not an ATM-only note.

RedotPay Pro, from the same chart, is 12.90 USD a month or 129 USD a year. It is optional. It is not the same product as a KAST membership.

| Transaction, deposit, and balance caps | No fixed maximum, with the page’s security and compliance caveats |

The memberships page adds who gets which cards. Standard includes 2 free cards, virtual or physical. Premium includes 1 Premium card plus the Standard allowance. Private includes 1 plated gold card plus 2 cards from the other tiers. Founders get Premium for life. After a missed payment there is a 7-day grace period, then the account moves to Standard. Upgrading from Premium to Private is not pro-rata.

KAST’s same fees page also prices some local rails. PIX in BRL is 1% including IOF, both ways, and the limits line says to talk to Concierge. SPEI in MXN is 0% for deposits and withdrawals, described as unlimited. ARS deposits are 0%. ARS withdrawals are 0% for Argentine residents and 1.6% including ITF for non-residents. Other payout currencies are on that page. This article does not copy the rest of the table.

Those PIX, SPEI, and ARS lines are **KAST’s**. RedotPay’s QR article does not print a RedotPay percentage for Pix or for a Latin American QR. Do not mix them.
