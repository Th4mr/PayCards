## Three kinds of card

1. **A spending app.** You deposit crypto, then spend it. RedotPay is this kind.
2. **An exchange card.** The card spends a balance you already keep on an exchange, such as Bybit Card or Crypto.com’s card. You avoid a separate deposit and you depend on that exchange.
3. **A multi-currency app.** Crypto and traditional balances in one product. Wirex is the usual example. What it offers in your country is on Wirex’s site, not here.

## What actually changes your result

- **Eligibility.** A card that is closed to your citizenship is not in the comparison. RedotPay’s list is in the [country guide](/en/articles/redotpay-supported-countries). The United States is on the citizenship block.
- **The fee for the way you pay.** Issuance, conversion, another currency, ATM cash.
- **How you fund it.** A transfer has a network fee. An exchange balance may not.
- **Wallets.** Apple Pay and Google Pay only count if the wallet accepts the card where you live.
- **Rewards.** Read the expiry and what the reward cannot pay. RedotPay’s 5 USD credit cannot pay the card fee.

## Where RedotPay fits, and where it does not

**Fits** when you want a spending app that is not your trading account, you can pass RedotPay’s KYC, and you have priced the 10 USD or 100 USD issuance plus the conversion rate that matches your payments. The card programmes named in the terms are Visa for Hong Kong and Singapore. Newer help pages say major card networks and 130 million+ merchants. Detail is in the [review](/en/articles/redotpay-review).

**Fits less** when your balance already sits on an exchange and that exchange will issue you a card. You skip a withdrawal and a network fee. You also put spending and trading in one company. If that company restricts your country, the card stops with it.

**Fits poorly** if you need cash and your address cannot receive the physical card, or if your citizenship is on the block list. A comparison table will not move either list.
