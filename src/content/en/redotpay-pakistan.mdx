This page is for English readers asking about Pakistan. It is not written for US residents. RedotPay does not issue a card to US residents. Nothing below is legal advice, and a missing country name is not permission to use a crypto card in Pakistan.

Checked on **8 November 2026**.

## What the four lists say about Pakistan

RedotPay does not publish a positive list of supported countries on the pages we read. It publishes block lists. Pakistan was not on any of these four when we re-read them on 8 November 2026:

- **Citizenship.** [Unsupported Countries/Regions](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/10339263-unsupported-countries-regions) was updated on **2 November 2026**. A citizen of a listed place cannot complete identity verification or use the service. The page names **35** places. Pakistan is not one of them. Albania, Croatia, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, and Slovenia were removed from this list on that update. They remain on the issuance list below, which is a different rule.
- **Location.** [Location-Based Access Restrictions](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/14252906-location-based-access-restrictions) is still dated **28 August 2026**. If you are located in a listed place, you cannot access the app. Pakistan is not on it. The United States is.
- **Card issuance.** [Card Issuance Restrictions](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/14254838-card-issuance-restrictions) is still dated **28 August 2026**. Residents of the listed areas are ineligible for a virtual card and a physical card, and the location cannot be a billing address. Pakistan is not on it. India is.
- **Delivery.** [Card Delivery Restrictions](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/14255319-card-delivery-restrictions) is still dated **21 July 2026**. It is about shipping the plastic card. Pakistan is not on it. Being absent here is not a promise that plastic will arrive.

The full copies are on the [country guide](/en/articles/redotpay-supported-countries). The app can still refuse a document or an address. Local law can restrict a crypto card even when the company would open the account.

## Opening an account

Account steps, the referral code, and the point that a code cannot be added after the account exists are in the [referral guide](/en/articles/redotpay-referral-code). The help pages we use for fees say the virtual card is **10 USD** once and the physical card is **100 USD** once, taken from the balance. The **5 USD** sign-up reward is spending credit. It does not pay the issuance fee. Sources for those amounts are on the [fees guide](/en/articles/redotpay-fees).

Do not pay either fee until the app shows the order as available. Identity verification is part of signup. The citizenship page does not name Pakistan, so that page does not apply its citizen block to a Pakistani citizen. That is not the same sentence as “the account will be approved.”

## Funding with USDT

A top-up is a crypto deposit, not a cash load in rupees. [How do I deposit crypto to my RedotPay account?](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/10339284-how-do-i-deposit-crypto-to-my-redotpay-account) is the path: open the wallet, tap Deposit, choose the asset and a network the app is showing, and copy that address.

[Supported cryptocurrencies, networks, and minimum deposits](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/10693364-supported-cryptocurrencies-networks-minimum-deposits) (21 July 2026) publishes the minimums as an image. For USDT the image lists ERC-20, BEP-20, opBNB, TRC20, Solana, TON, and Arbitrum One, each with a minimum of **1.00 USDT**. A deposit under the minimum is not credited. A later deposit of the same asset on the same network can be combined with it. The deposit article does not describe a wrong-network transfer as combinable. The steps and the rest of the image are in [how to top up](/en/articles/how-to-top-up-redotpay).

The services page dated 24 July 2026 also lists USDC, BTC, ETH, BNB, SOL, TRX, XRP, SUI, and S. Use an asset the Deposit screen offers. This page does not add a Pakistan-only rail.

## Send and PKR

[RedotPay Send](/en/articles/redotpay-send) turns crypto in the balance, except USDs, into local currency for a personal bank account or e-wallet. Business accounts are not supported. The international-transfer articles do not print one fee for every currency. The fee and the amount the recipient gets are on the Send screen before you confirm.

PKR is named on a different page. [How do I use a promo code?](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/17693143-how-do-i-use-a-promo-code), read on 8 November 2026, says a promo code reduces the transfer fee, not the amount you send. You can save up to **20 USD**, and the fee can drop to 0. Someone else’s code can be used on your first send. You cannot use your own. After your first send in a supported currency the page names — **EUR, GBP, USD, MYR, BRL, PKR, AED, THB, INR** — your code appears on the order details. PKR is in that list. The page does not print a PKR fee, a PKR minimum, or a processing time. If the Send screen does not offer PKR, do not treat this sentence as a substitute.

The signup referral code on this site is not that Send promo code.

## P2P, only if the app lists it

[Which countries can use RedotPay P2P?](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/12042138-which-countries-can-use-redotpay-p2p) is dated **5 August 2026**. It says RedotPay P2P is currently available in over 100 countries and regions, including Angola, Andorra, Argentina, Australia, Austria, Bahrain, Bangladesh, Belgium, Bulgaria, and the United Arab Emirates. **Pakistan is not in that named sample.** The page says availability is updated regularly and that the current list is in the app. If P2P is available where you are, you can start an order. If it is not in your app, this guide does not invent a rupee purchase inside RedotPay.

## What Pakistan’s regulator publishes

The [PVARA licensing page](https://www.pvara.gov.pk/licensing), read on 8 November 2026, says the Virtual Assets Act, 2026 requires all Virtual Asset Service Providers to obtain a formal licence before offering services in Pakistan. It says PVARA accepts applications for the Regulatory Sandbox, No Objection Certificates, and VASP Licences. The same page says its FAQ is not legal advice and that the Act and the Regulations prevail over anything stated there.

One FAQ answer says PVARA does not approve, endorse, or guarantee any virtual asset. A notification to the Authority in connection with a listing does not constitute approval of the virtual asset.

The licensing page does not name RedotPay. This guide does not say that RedotPay holds a PVARA licence, and it does not say that RedotPay lacks one. Whether a service needs a licence, and whether a particular company has one, is a question for the Act and for PVARA, not a conclusion this page can draw from a missing name on a company block list.
